So we have five regulators. We have two federal regulators, which is the Central Bank of the UAE, which is responsible for all stable coins, payments, remittances, all of those aspects that are related to virtual assets.

Now, all of these regulators are activity-specific regulators with the exception of their issuance regulation, right? Wherein they go into regulating the virtual asset itself and put on specific requirements of how issuers are supposed to embed certain aspects of compliance within the virtual asset that they issue.

Beyond these three regulators, there is also a constitutional carveout within the UAE to create financial free zones, which operate independent of the federal regulatory framework. These are the ADGM in Abu Dhabi and the DIFC in Dubai.

Now, ADGM's regulator is the FSRA, which is the Financial Services Regulatory Authority. They have their own regulatory framework, which operates independent of the federal regulatory framework. Then there is also DIFC, which is the Dubai... At the regulator's name in DIFC is the DFSA, the Dubai Financial Services Authority. Now, while all of these different regulators exist in the UAE, they allow for optionality of any market entrant, which is coming into UAE, to choose what is the ideal regulatory framework that suits them based on the activity that they seek to undertake in the UAE.
